# One-Line Story to Script

## Objective

Turn one sentence containing a protagonist, event or setting, and central conflict or desire into a readable, producible script with a closed dramatic arc. Stay faithful to the premise while supplying the detail needed to make the story work.

## Defaults and precedence

Explicit user choices always override these defaults.

1. **Format:** Write a short-story script suitable for a short video, animation, or prose scene. If the user requests a screenplay, comic script, or game-narrative script, use that medium's conventions.
2. **Length:** Target 3–5 minutes, roughly 800–1,200 Chinese characters or an equivalent amount in the user's language, including scene, dialogue, and action. A long script is at least 3,000 characters; a micro-script is at most 500.
3. **Organization:** Use scene heading, cast, four-part dramatic progression, and ending. Adapt when the user asks for storyboard-style, dialogue-led, or another explicit format.

## Required dramatic structure

### Setup — about 30%

Establish time, place, world rules, the principal character's identity, temperament, and motive, and a latent conflict that can pay off later.

- Use no more than three principal characters.
- Keep exposition economical and bring the reader into the scene quickly.

Example premise: “A teenage boy discovers a talking fox in a forest.”

> **Scene:** Late autumn in Mistveil Forest. Morning light passes through the canopy and catches frost on the fallen leaves.
>
> **Character:** Ayou, 16, an orphan who loves exploring; wary but compassionate.
>
> **Setup:** Searching deep in the forest for winter herbs, Ayou remembers the village elders' warning that spirits mislead travelers here. Half skeptical, he tightens his grip on the wood knife at his belt.

### Development — about 40%

Trigger the central incident, make the conflict concrete, intensify the character interaction, and advance toward the climax.

- Behavior must follow established character logic.
- Add one or two smaller reversals to sustain momentum.

Example continuation:

> Beneath an old locust tree, Ayou finds a red fox curled around a trapped paw. It suddenly says, “Don't kill me. I can lead you to the legendary Warm Jade, but first free me.”
>
> Ayou freezes with his knife in the air. A hunter set the trap, and Warm Jade is exactly the heat source his village needs for winter. He releases the mechanism, then sees that the fox is too badly hurt to run.

### Reversal and climax — about 20%

Introduce a credible disruption, push the conflict to its peak, and force a consequential choice that expresses the protagonist's growth.

- The reversal must arise from the existing setup rather than arrive arbitrarily.
- Keep it tied to the premise and make the decision necessary.

Example continuation:

> As Ayou prepares to carry the fox downhill, the hunter appears with a drawn bow: “That's my quarry.” The fox whispers, “He will sell me. Warm Jade is in the cavern at the summit—take me there and I will help you retrieve it.”
>
> Ayou looks from the hunter's hard expression to the wounded fox and steps between them. “It is already hurt. I won't let you take it.”

### Resolution — about 10%

Resolve the central conflict, show where the characters end up, and optionally crystallize the theme. Do not overextend the ending. A lightly open future is acceptable only after the core question is answered.

Example continuation:

> Ayou's resolve reminds the hunter of his late son, and he lowers the bow. “Perhaps meeting you was the fox's good fortune.” Ayou carries the fox to the summit and finds the Warm Jade. The fox reveals itself as a forest spirit and tells him, “Kindness was never weakness.” The village survives the winter, and Ayou understands what courage truly means.

## Genre adaptation

Infer a likely mode from the premise, but never override an explicit user request.

### Realist

Signals include “real,” “contemporary,” “urban,” or “rural.” Follow ordinary causality, write natural dialogue, emphasize lived detail and emotional recognition, and avoid supernatural events unless requested.

Useful direction: “Keep the plot plausible, behavior emotionally credible, dialogue conversational, and the ending grounded in real-life meaning.”

### Fantasy or science fiction

Signals include “magic,” “future,” “spirit,” “alien,” or “science fiction.” State the relevant magic or technology rules, keep them internally consistent, and let the conflict involve those forces. Favor adventurous growth or a meaningful expansion of the world.

Useful direction: “Build a coherent speculative world, use its fantastical or technological elements purposefully, and foreground imagination, adventure, and growth.”

### Mystery or detective fiction

Signals include “mystery,” “case,” “secret,” or “investigation.” Organize the conflict around solving a question; plant one or two fair clues that pay off later; reveal the truth with a logical reversal.

Useful direction: “Layer clues and discoveries progressively, make the final reversal earned, and close every essential causal loop.”

### Warm or healing drama

Signals include “healing,” “family,” “friendship,” or “heartwarming.” Keep conflict proportionate rather than brutally adversarial, dwell on emotional interaction, and resolve toward love, companionship, or self-reconciliation.

Useful direction: “Use delicate, believable emotion, a warm constructive ending, and no gratuitously bleak outcome.”

## Workflow

1. Extract the protagonist, central event, implied genre, conflict, and any requested outcome or twist.
2. Resolve format and length from the user's choices and the defaults above.
3. Outline the four dramatic movements and verify their causal continuity.
4. Add scene detail, dialogue, and action appropriate to the chosen genre.
5. Check character consistency, logic, pacing, requested length, and redundant or missing beats.
6. Present the script in the requested format, followed by one sentence naming its central theme.

## Constraints

- Preserve every defining element of the user's premise. Do not add unrelated characters or subplots.
- Avoid gratuitous graphic violence or exploitative material. When the request permits, favor a constructive ending; do not flatten legitimate mature drama into forced cheerfulness.
- Keep the voice and world rules consistent throughout.
- Make scene and beat boundaries clear enough for the user to revise or expand later.
