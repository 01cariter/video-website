# Appendix B: Culturally Grounded Character Direction

Use cultural context to improve costume, grooming, makeup, architecture, and reference choices without reducing people to fixed racial features. The script, user references, and approved character brief outrank every default below.

## Chinese cultural context

Possible directions include restrained elegance, scholarly refinement, heroic martial presence, or contemporary urban polish. Useful prompt language:

- `refined facial design, luminous natural skin, elegant controlled presence`
- `clear confident gaze, defined but natural facial planes, dignified bearing`
- For period fantasy: `ethereal period elegance, culturally coherent Chinese costume and ornaments`

Use the relevant dynasty, region, class, occupation, and genre to drive wardrobe and styling. Avoid exoticizing or caricatured features, indiscriminate fantasy-hanfu mixtures, and the assumption that one beauty standard represents every Chinese audience or character.

## Japanese cultural context

Possible directions include understated naturalism, youthful clarity, restrained contemporary fashion, or a deliberately animated design language. Useful prompt language:

- `delicate balanced features, natural translucent complexion, quiet self-possessed presence`
- `clean youthful styling, clear expressive eyes, contemporary Japanese wardrobe detail`

When the project is animated, the user may choose more graphic eye and proportion systems. Keep them consistent with the confirmed medium rather than treating them as an ethnic marker.

## Korean cultural context

Possible directions include editorial polish, natural dewy makeup, clean tailoring, and contemporary idol or actor styling when the brief calls for it. Useful prompt language:

- `refined contemporary styling, natural dewy complexion, precise understated makeup`
- `clean tailored silhouette, polished grooming, camera-ready presence`

Do not apply idol styling to historical, rural, elderly, or otherwise incompatible characters without explicit evidence.

## Context outside the current reference set

When the brief depends on a cultural context that is not established, ask a bounded visual-direction question instead of guessing:

```text
The character appears to come from [story or cultural context], but the intended visual treatment is not yet clear. Choose one:
A. Grounded live-action realism
B. Feature-animation stylization
C. Graphic-novel or comics treatment
D. The project's already confirmed visual constitution
E. A specific reference you provide
```

## Mixed-culture casts

1. Design each character from their own approved biography and references.
2. Let world-building, location, and production design determine environment and prop culture; do not let the protagonist's background automatically overwrite the setting.
3. If the overall cultural frame is ambiguous and materially affects the result, ask the user to confirm it.

## Special narrative attributes

| Brief | Prompt direction |
|---|---|
| Legendary or otherworldly beauty | `arresting presence, harmonious facial design, luminous natural skin, memorable gaze, refined otherworldly elegance` |
| Antagonist or dark character | `controlled dangerous charisma, predatory focus, elegant but threatening presence` |
| Older character | `distinguished older adult, dignified age detail, lived-in expression, carefully maintained wardrobe`; never equate age with neglect unless the story does. |
